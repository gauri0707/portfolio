CFashion — a journey through the PM lifecycle

Fashion recommendations for color-blind users, built end-to-end with a small team.

  1. 01

    The Spark

    300 million people worldwide live with color vision deficiency. Fashion, it turns out, is one of the few places nobody had built anything for them.

  2. 02

    Getting Out of the Building

    Before writing a line of code: secondary research, a 130-person survey (10% colorblind), and interviews with both a colorblind student and an industry expert at Oracle.

  3. 03

    Naming the Problem

    Six distinct pain points, boiled into one problem statement. Even the literature had gaps — existing color models topped out at 865 colors against the textile industry's 1,000+, and nobody validated recommendations at the outfit level.

  4. 04

    Designing & Building the System

    Grounded in real color theory: three modules — segmentation (Mask R-CNN), color compatibility (ML model + color wheel + industry-standard TCX codes), and outfit recommendation. Built on 2,000 images we scraped and hand-labeled ourselves.

  5. 05

    Shipping Smart, Not Perfect

    Rather than wait for a critical mass of users, we shipped content-based recommendations first — works from day one — with a clear path to collaborative filtering once there's real usage data.

  6. 06

    Proof It Works

    Object detection at 78–98% confidence on real street photos, and a working app: Scan Outfit → Suitable Colors with % match → Style Outfit. Bonus case: the fashion industry wastes 92M tons of textiles a year — a recommendation engine that gets it right the first time is also a sustainability story.

Presented at the Aavishkar Inter-Collegiate Research Convention.
