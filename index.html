CFashion: Outfit Recommendations

Product Requirements Document

Problem

Color-blind users (300M+ globally) can't reliably identify complementary colors when shopping. No existing product addresses this in fashion, and the few color models that exist stop at 865 colors against the textile industry's 1,000+.

Users

Color-blind shoppers who choose their own outfits (primary). Friends and family who currently help them decide what matches (secondary).

Success metrics

Garment detection at 90% confidence or higher on real street photos. Color matching that covers 1,000+ textile colors. A full Scan Outfit, Suitable Colors, Style Outfit flow that needs no help from a sighted person.

Requirements
  • P0 Segment top, bottom and accessories from a photo at 90% confidence or higher
  • P0 Color-wheel and TCX-code compatibility mapping across 1,000+ textile colors
  • P0 Top and bottom match score, shown as a percentage on every suggestion
  • P1 Content-based recommendations that work from day one, with no usage history needed
Risks

Small training set: 2,000 hand-labeled images, so confidence is checked on real street photos, not only the training data. No usage data yet: ship content-based recommendations first and add collaborative filtering once there is real usage.

Out of scope v1

Collaborative filtering, AR try-on

AgentAssist: RAG Copilot for Support Agents

Product Requirements Document · Sample

Problem

Tier-1 agents search five different knowledge bases during every live chat. Handle time stays high, and two agents often give two different answers to the same question.

Users

Tier-1 support agents (primary). Team leads and QA analysts who review conversations (secondary).

Success metrics

Average handle time down 15%. First-contact resolution up 5 points. At least 70% of suggested answers accepted by agents.

Requirements
  • P0 Answer only from approved articles and show the source link next to every answer
  • P0 Respond in under 3 seconds inside the live chat window
  • P0 One-click thumbs up or down on each answer, reviewed weekly by the content team
  • P1 Auto-generated handoff summary when a chat moves to tier 2
Risks

Wrong answers: show "no confident answer" below a retrieval threshold and fall back to plain search. Stale articles: the knowledge base team owns a weekly review of low-rated answers.

Out of scope v1

Voice calls, auto-sending replies to customers, multilingual support

PantryPal: Cut Household Food Waste

Product Requirements Document · Sample

Problem

Households throw away roughly a third of the food they buy, mostly because they forget what is in the fridge and when it expires. Existing grocery apps help people buy, not use what they already have.

Users

Busy two-income households and shared student flats that grocery shop once a week.

Success metrics

30% fewer discarded items per household after 8 weeks. 40% of users still active in week 4. Under 10 seconds to add a full grocery trip.

Requirements
  • P0 Add items by scanning a receipt or barcode, with manual entry as a fallback
  • P0 Reminder 2 days before expiry, paired with a recipe that uses the item
  • P0 Shared household pantry so two people always see the same list
  • P1 Weekly summary of food saved and money saved
Risks

Data entry fatigue: scanning is the default path. Notification fatigue: cap reminders at one digest per day, and let users pick the time.

Out of scope v1

Grocery delivery integration, nutrition tracking, smart fridge hardware

Nivara: building a smart tent from zero

A tent that opens its own vents and turns on fans when you get too warm. Problem, prototypes, real campers, a trade show.

  • Product

From a hot night to a trade show floor

Six chapters, each one backed by something we made or measured.

01

The spark

Tents trap heat, and nobody wants to unzip a vent at 2am

Traditional tents rely on passive ventilation, so body heat and humidity build up and sleep suffers. Manual vents mean getting up, fighting zippers and guessing.

02

Validate before building

We asked real campers first

Surveys plus on-the-ground testing at the Duke Campout event and Instagram reach-outs. Campers also asked for solar charging and LED indicators, which went on the roadmap.

How often do you feel uncomfortable in a tent? Always 21% · Often 26% · Sometimes 42% · Rarely or never 11%

03

Build

Four prototype generations, then packaging

From a plywood shutter to a fan module mounted on a real tent, with a motor, gears, two temperature sensors and a custom circuit board.

04

Prove it works

Heat pack, ice pack, and four test cases

A bench test used a heat pack and an ice pack to simulate hot and cold, inside and outside. All four conditions behaved as expected: vent open and fan on when it needed cooling, closed and off when it was cold.

05

Market it

Who it is for and where it fits

Three personas, a market sized from top to bottom, and a crowded field with room for a niche smart tent. The North Face, Big Agnes and NEMO dominate, but the market is fragmented.

06

Sell

A trade show, a value chain, and a path to customers

Our go-to-market plan: buy standard tents in bulk, refactor them with Nivara’s vent module, then sell on Amazon, Walmart and Target and on a direct-to-consumer site. We presented the prototype live at a trade show.
